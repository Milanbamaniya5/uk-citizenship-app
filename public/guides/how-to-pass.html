How to Pass the Life in the UK Test: A 4-Week Revision Plan

Last updated: October 2026

Most people who fail the test did not study too little. They studied in the wrong way, for example by reading the whole handbook once and hoping for the best. This plan gives you a structure you can follow in about four weeks, studying 30 to 45 minutes a day.

Week 1: History

Read our British history timeline first, then the history chapters in the handbook. Make a one-page list of key dates and the person or event linked to each. Do not try to memorise every detail. Focus on the major turning points: the Romans, 1066, Magna Carta, the Tudors, the Civil War, the Industrial Revolution and the two World Wars.

Week 2: Government and law

Study how Parliament works and how the legal system works. These topics contain many numbers, such as the number of MPs, the age to vote and the size of a jury. Put each number on a flashcard. Take Practice Test 1 at the end of the week.

Week 3: Traditions and culture

Read about festivals, national days and patron saints, then the sections on sport, art, literature and science. Questions here often ask which person is linked to which achievement. Take Practice Test 2 and review every wrong answer.

Week 4: Practice and review

Take Practice Tests 3 and 4, then go back to the tests you did earlier. If you score 20 or more regularly, you are ready. If you keep missing the same topic, spend a day re-reading that chapter. Rest the evening before the test.

Tips for answering questions

  • Read the whole question. Words such as "not" or "first" change the answer.
  • Remove the options you know are wrong, then choose between those left.
  • Be careful with similar-sounding names and dates, such as the Act of Union and the Bill of Rights.
  • Do not spend too long on one question. You can return to it before you finish.

On the day

Take your ID, arrive early and read each instruction on the screen. Most candidates find the test easier than expected if they have practised in the same format.
