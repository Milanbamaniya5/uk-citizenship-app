UK Traditions, Festivals and Patron Saints

Last updated: October 2026

Many test questions ask about customs, holidays and national symbols. Use this guide to learn the key dates in the year.

National saints and emblems

  • England: St George, 23 April. Emblem: the rose.
  • Scotland: St Andrew, 30 November. Emblem: the thistle.
  • Wales: St David, 1 March. Emblems: the daffodil and the leek.
  • Northern Ireland: St Patrick, 17 March. Emblem: the shamrock.

The year in festivals

  • 1 January: New Year's Day. Scotland celebrates Hogmanay on 31 December and often keeps the holiday on 2 January too.
  • 25 January: Burns Night, honouring the Scottish poet Robert Burns.
  • Shrove Tuesday: Pancake Day, the day before Lent begins.
  • Easter: Good Friday and Easter Monday are public holidays. Many people eat hot cross buns and chocolate eggs.
  • May and August: Bank holidays give people long weekends.
  • 31 October: Halloween.
  • 5 November: Guy Fawkes Night, with bonfires and fireworks, recalling the Gunpowder Plot of 1605.
  • Second Sunday of November: Remembrance Sunday. People wear red poppies and observe two minutes of silence.
  • 25 December: Christmas Day. 26 December: Boxing Day.

Festivals of other faiths

The UK is home to many faiths. Diwali, the festival of lights, is celebrated by Hindus, Sikhs and Jains. Eid marks the end of Ramadan and the pilgrimage season for Muslims. Hanukkah is a Jewish festival of lights, and Vaisakhi is an important Sikh festival.

Arts and culture

The Edinburgh Festival Fringe is one of the world's largest arts festivals. The National Eisteddfod celebrates Welsh language, music and poetry. The Notting Hill Carnival, held on the August bank holiday weekend, celebrates Caribbean culture. Wimbledon, played on grass in south-west London, is one of the best-known sporting events.
