How Government and Parliament Work in the UK

Last updated: October 2026

The United Kingdom is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy. That means the King or Queen is head of state, but elected politicians make the laws and run the country. This guide explains the key parts of the system.

The Monarch

The monarch is the head of state but does not make political decisions. The monarch opens each new session of Parliament and formally appoints the Prime Minister, who is the leader of the party that can command a majority in the House of Commons.

The House of Commons

The Commons is the elected part of Parliament. It has 650 Members of Parliament (MPs), each representing a constituency. The Speaker chairs debates and must stay politically neutral. MPs debate and vote on new laws, and they question ministers.

The House of Lords

The Lords is the second chamber. It examines and suggests changes to proposed laws but cannot permanently block laws passed by the Commons. Its members are not elected in general elections.

How a law is made

A proposed law is called a bill. It is debated and voted on in both Houses, and when it has been agreed it receives Royal Assent from the monarch and becomes an Act of Parliament.

The Prime Minister and the Cabinet

The Prime Minister chooses senior ministers to form the Cabinet. Examples are the Chancellor of the Exchequer (finance), the Home Secretary (policing and immigration) and the Foreign Secretary. The Prime Minister lives at 10 Downing Street and has a country house at Chequers. The largest party that is not in government forms the Opposition, and its leader is the Leader of the Opposition.

Elections

A general election must be held at least every five years. People aged 18 or over who are registered can vote. Each constituency elects one MP using the first past the post system: the candidate with the most votes wins. In Scotland and Wales, people can vote from age 16 in devolved elections.

Devolution

Some powers are devolved to national bodies: the Scottish Parliament at Holyrood in Edinburgh, the Senedd (Welsh Parliament) in Cardiff, and the Northern Ireland Assembly at Stormont in Belfast. They make decisions on matters such as health and education in their own nation.

Local government

Councils are responsible for local services such as refuse collection, libraries, planning and some social care. Councillors are elected in local elections.
