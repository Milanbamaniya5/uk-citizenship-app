Law, Rights and Everyday Life in the UK

Last updated: October 2026

The Life in the UK test asks about the legal system and about the rights and responsibilities of people who live in the UK. This guide covers the essentials.

Rights and freedoms

People in the UK have important freedoms, including freedom of speech, freedom of religion and belief, and the right to a fair trial. The Human Rights Act 1998 brought the European Convention on Human Rights into UK law. The Equality Act 2010 protects people from discrimination because of characteristics such as age, disability, race, religion, sex and sexual orientation.

Responsibilities

  • Obey the law and pay the taxes you owe.
  • Respect the rights of other people, including their right to their own opinions.
  • Look after your family and treat other people with respect.
  • Attend jury service if you are called.

Courts

Minor criminal cases in England and Wales are heard by magistrates. Serious cases go to the Crown Court, where a judge directs a jury of 12 people. Scotland has its own legal system. A Scottish jury has 15 members, and the verdict can be guilty, not guilty or not proven. Jury members must be aged between 18 and 75 and registered to vote.

Police and emergencies

Police officers protect the public and investigate crime. Call 999 in an emergency, or 112, which also works. For urgent medical advice that is not an emergency, call 111. For crimes that are not urgent, you can call 101.

Important age limits

  • 5 to 16: full-time education is compulsory in England. After 16, young people must stay in education or training until 18.
  • 17: the minimum age to drive a car.
  • 18: the age to vote in general elections, serve on a jury, and buy alcohol or tobacco.

Health and public services

The National Health Service (NHS) was founded in 1948 and is mostly free to use for people who are ordinarily resident in the UK. You register with a local doctor, called a GP, to get routine care.
